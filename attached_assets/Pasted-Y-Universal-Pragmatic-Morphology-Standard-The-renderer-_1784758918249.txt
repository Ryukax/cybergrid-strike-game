Universal Pragmatic Morphology Standard
The renderer must stop generating arbitrary silhouettes.
Every entity should be constructed from a functional body plan determined by its actual behavior, movement, offense, defense, and role.
The rendering pipeline should become:
Gameplay Traits
    ↓
Functional Role
    ↓
Locomotion Model
    ↓
Attack Delivery Model
    ↓
Defense / Armor Model
    ↓
Mass Distribution
    ↓
Primary Chassis
    ↓
Functional Structures
    ↓
Secondary Adaptations
    ↓
Surface Details
Not:
Traits
    ↓
Random body architecture
    ↓
Decorative appendages
1. Every visible structure must have a reason to exist
Do not add spikes, lobes, fins, tentacles, wings, or protrusions merely for visual variety.
A structure should correspond to an actual function.
Examples:
fast lateral movement
→ narrow streamlined body
→ rear propulsion structure
→ stabilizing fins

heavy armor
→ broad compact chassis
→ layered exterior plating
→ protected central core

long-range projectile attack
→ forward-facing barrel / emitter / proboscis
→ recoil-supporting body mass
→ directional silhouette

radial projectile attack
→ central core
→ multiple clearly oriented emitter nodes

ramming behavior
→ reinforced frontal wedge / horn
→ mass concentrated toward front

high maneuverability
→ multiple articulated propulsion or control surfaces
→ compact center of mass

shielding
→ visible external shell, field projector nodes, or barrier frame

carrier/spawner
→ enlarged central body cavity
→ brood chambers, ports, or detachable pods

stationary turret
→ anchored base
→ rotating or directional weapon structure

sniper
→ elongated axis
→ obvious forward weapon
→ minimal lateral mass

swarm unit
→ small compact chassis
→ simplified anatomy optimized for replication
The player should be able to infer an enemy’s approximate role from its silhouette before seeing it attack.

Most important change for your current code
I would specifically say:
Remove the assumption that silhouette diversity is achieved through continuous perimeter deformation. The screenshots demonstrate that this produces flower-like, amoeboid, and randomly lobed entities regardless of architecture. Rebuild morphology as an assembly of discrete functional anatomical components attached to a coherent chassis with a defined forward axis. Gameplay traits must determine component presence, size, position, orientation, and mass allocation. Randomness may modify proportions but must never determine the fundamental anatomy. At gameplay scale, each entity must visibly communicate movement system, attack system, defensive system, and directional orientation. Delete or heavily restrict arbitrary radial lobes and freeform Bézier protrusions.
And I would add one very explicit acceptance test:
Before accepting the implementation, render every morphology in monochrome with glow, color, texture, animation, and internal details disabled. If the role and facing direction cannot still be distinguished from silhouette alone, the morphology has failed.
That is the pragmatic step the current version is missing. The screenshots indicate the architecture system is technically more varied, but perceptually it is still one generator: irregular central blob + lobes. The next rewrite needs to change the underlying representation, not tune the Bézier curves again.
