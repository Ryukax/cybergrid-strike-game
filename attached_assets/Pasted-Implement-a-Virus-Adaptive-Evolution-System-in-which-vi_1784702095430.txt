Implement a Virus Adaptive Evolution System in which virus populations consistently evolve toward compositions better suited to the conditions encountered as waves progress.

Core Principle

Virus morphology is not static and should not evolve through arbitrary tier upgrades. Each successive wave should apply evolutionary pressure based on:

* Player behavior and combat style
* Damage types received
* Weapons and abilities used against viruses
* Virus survival duration
* Successful attacks against the player
* Environmental or map conditions
* Movement requirements
* Population performance of previous morphologies
* Current wave difficulty and progression

The game should continuously track which virus traits perform well and use that information to influence the composition of future generations.

Adaptive Trait Model

Represent every virus as a composition of independently variable traits, such as:

* body structure
* size
* armor
* density
* speed
* acceleration
* maneuverability
* attack range
* attack frequency
* projectile behavior
* regeneration
* resistance
* sensory/detection radius
* aggression
* evasiveness
* replication behavior
* special abilities

These traits should be universally combinable rather than tied to fixed named classes.

A virus therefore evolves by modifying its trait composition, allowing entirely different functional forms to emerge from the same underlying system.

Evolution Between Waves

At the end of each wave:

1. Evaluate the performance of every virus phenotype.
2. Determine which traits contributed to survival, damage dealt, successful positioning, or other useful outcomes.
3. Increase the probability that successful traits appear in subsequent waves.
4. Reduce the probability of repeatedly unsuccessful traits.
5. Introduce controlled mutation so new configurations can emerge.
6. Allow combinations of successful traits to produce hybrid descendants.
7. Preserve enough variation that the population does not converge permanently on a single optimal form.

The next wave should therefore be generated approximately as:

Next Population = Selection(Previous Population) + Mutation + Recombination + Difficulty Pressure

Counter-Adaptation

The system should recognize persistent player strategies.

Examples:

* Repeated ranged attacks favor faster, evasive, shielded, or projectile-resistant viruses.
* Heavy close-range damage favors spacing behavior, armor, knockback resistance, or ranged attacks.
* Highly mobile players favor predictive attacks, greater tracking capability, area denial, or interception behavior.
* Repeated use of one damage type gradually favors resistance to that damage type.
* Defensive player behavior may favor siege, swarming, penetration, or sustained-pressure traits.

Adaptation should be gradual rather than instantaneous. A player strategy should create evolutionary pressure over several encounters before becoming strongly reflected in the population.

Population-Level Evolution

Do not make every virus evolve identically.

Each wave should contain an ecosystem of related variants:

* dominant adaptations that address current conditions
* specialized variants exploiting particular player weaknesses
* generalist variants
* experimental mutations
* occasional ancestral or previously successful forms

Successful mutations can become increasingly common over later waves.

This allows the population itself to evolve while preserving meaningful visual and mechanical diversity.

Morphology Integration

The existing Virus Morphology Encoding Standard should visually encode these evolved properties.

A virus’s appearance must be generated from its actual functional composition wherever practical.

For example:

* armor changes structural thickness or plating
* speed produces more streamlined or directional geometry
* sensory capability affects antennae, eyes, or detection structures
* attack specialization alters appendages or weapon-like structures
* regeneration creates visibly organic or redundant structures
* defensive specialization creates compact or reinforced morphology

The exact visual mappings should remain universal and procedural rather than relying on specific hard-coded archetypes.

Progression Pressure

Wave number should increase the evolutionary search space rather than simply multiplying health and damage.

Earlier waves:

* fewer active traits
* simpler combinations
* larger mutation constraints

Later waves:

* more simultaneous traits
* stronger specialization
* greater hybridization
* increasingly sophisticated counter-adaptation
* access to rare structural mutations and emergent compositions

Difficulty scaling may still increase baseline statistics, but the primary progression mechanism should be increasing adaptive suitability and compositional complexity.

Anti-Stagnation Rules

Prevent evolution from collapsing into one permanently dominant build.

Use:

* mutation floors
* diminishing returns on excessive specialization
* ecological niches
* population diversity requirements
* changing selection pressures
* counter-traits
* occasional disruptive mutations

The intended result is that the player should be able to observe the virus population becoming progressively better adapted to both the player and the environment.

By later waves, viruses should feel like descendants of earlier populations that have undergone sustained artificial evolution—not merely stronger enemies spawned from a difficulty table.
